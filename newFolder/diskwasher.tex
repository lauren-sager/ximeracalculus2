\begin{exercise}
In the figure below you will see two different area profiles for functions $f$ and $g$, respectively. 
    \begin{image}
    \includegraphics{Recitation Worksheets/Images/6.3cone.png}
    \end{image}

    \begin{image}
    \includegraphics{Recitation Worksheets/Images/6.3disks2.png}
    \end{image}
If you rotate the curves $f$ and $g$ around the $x$-axis they will scoop out cone-shaped or ``horn''-shaped volumes with the same radius and height. Draw a sketch of each solid, and a representative cross-section. (Visualization hint: The cones all point to the right.)\\
\begin{enumerate}
\item Choose a constant value $a>0$ and set $r = h = a$. Determine the formula for $f(x)$ so that it follows the profile in the first picture. Formulate the volume of the region obtained by rotating $f$ around the $x$-axis using the disk method. Evaluate your integral and verify that the numerical value of the volume agrees with the cone volume formula for $r$ and $h$.
\vspace{1.25in}
\item Using the same choice of $r=h=a$, the formula for $g(x)=\frac{1}{a}(x-a)^2$.  Formulate the volume of the region obtained by rotating $g$ around the $x$-axis using the disk method.  Evaluate your integral, and compare your answer with the cone volume in (a).
\vspace{1.25in}
\item Also formulate the volumes of the regions formed by rotating $f$ and $g$ around the $y$-axis using the disk method.  Compare your answers with (a) and (b). (Hint: you will need to reformulate the equations for $f$ and $g$ into functions of $y$. Think about the part of the $g$ curve that is actually rotating.)
\vspace{1.25in}
\item Formulate the volume of the region formed by rotating $f$ around the line $x=h$.  Evaluate your integral.

\end{enumerate}
